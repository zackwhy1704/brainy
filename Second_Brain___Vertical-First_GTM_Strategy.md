# Second Brain — Vertical-First GTM Strategy

## 1. Executive Summary

### The strategic thesis

Build a **horizontal intelligence platform underneath**, but go to market with **one extremely specific professional problem**.

The platform should ultimately support:

- Executive recruiters
- Property agents
- Consultants
- Sales professionals
- Other relationship-driven professionals

But the first customer should be:

# Executive Recruiters

The initial product should be positioned as:

> **AI relationship memory for executive recruiters.**

Not:

> AI second brain

Not:

> AI note taker

Not:

> AI CRM

Not:

> AI recruiting agent

The broader **Second Brain** concept should remain the technology and long-term vision.

The initial commercial wedge is:

> **Before you contact a candidate, know everything that matters — and where you learned it.**

The product captures fragmented information from conversations, notes, resumes, voice messages, screenshots, emails and other sources, then maintains a living, source-backed understanding of each candidate.

---

# 2. Why Vertical-First Is Necessary

A horizontal product has an attractive vision:

> "An AI that understands everything on your phone."

But it creates three GTM problems.

### Problem 1 — Weak immediate value proposition

"Second brain" is conceptually interesting but doesn't immediately answer:

> Why should I install this?

### Problem 2 — Commodity perception

Generic AI summarization, note-taking and personal memory are rapidly becoming platform features.

Apple, Google, OpenAI and dedicated AI productivity companies can all compete around generic summarization and memory.

### Problem 3 — No obvious acquisition channel

"Everyone who uses a phone" is not a customer segment.

Recruiters are.

Property agents are.

Consultants are.

Salespeople are.

That gives you a concrete audience to make videos for, contact directly, interview and build around.

---

# 3. The Strategic Model

The company should operate on three layers:

```text
                    LONG-TERM VISION
                   SECOND BRAIN ENGINE
                           │
                           ▼
                 RELATIONSHIP INTELLIGENCE
                           │
          ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
          │                │                │
          ▼                ▼                ▼
    Candidate Memory   Client Memory   Customer Memory
          │                │                │
          ▼                ▼                ▼
      RECRUITING        CONSULTING        SALES
          │
```

The mistake would be building each vertical as a completely separate product.

Instead, build a reusable intelligence engine and put a vertical-specific **ontology + workflows + UX + marketing proposition** on top.

# 6. Why Executive Recruiting Is the First Wedge

Executive recruitment is especially attractive because the product can improve something extremely valuable:

## Relationship continuity.

A recruiter may know:

> Sarah is a CFO.

But that's not enough.

The valuable information is:

> Sarah wants regional responsibility.

> She isn't interested in PE-backed companies.

> She wants approximately $350–400k.

> She doesn't want to relocate.

> She is interested in fintech.

> She rejected the previous opportunity because the scope was too narrow.

> She said she may reconsider after her board meeting.

That information often exists across:

- WhatsApp
- phone calls
- email
- LinkedIn
- meeting notes
- personal memory
- spreadsheets
- ATS notes

The product's job is to turn those fragments into **relationship intelligence**.

# 7. Do NOT Build Another Recruiting CRM

This distinction is critical.

Current recruiting platforms already offer increasingly sophisticated CRM and AI capabilities.

Loxo positions its product as a full recruiting CRM/talent-intelligence platform with candidate history, AI matching, outreach and workflow automation.

Recruiterflow similarly offers an AI-native CRM with agents that remember interactions, update records, set reminders, schedule interviews and perform research.

Therefore:

## Do not compete on:

- ATS
- pipeline management
- candidate sourcing
- bulk outreach
- resume database
- generic CRM
- recruitment automation

Those are already crowded.

Instead:

# Become the intelligence layer around the recruiter's existing workflow.

# 9. The "Shows Its Receipts" Principle

AI-generated relationship intelligence can easily become untrustworthy.

Therefore every meaningful claim should be:

### AI-generated

but

### source-backed.

Example:

> **Sarah is more open to moving than previously.**

[View evidence]

→ WhatsApp conversation  
→ Aug 17  
→ Relevant message

This becomes a product principle:

# Intelligence without provenance is just AI guessing.

# 28. Interview Questions

Do not ask:

> "Would you use an AI second brain?"

You will get useless answers.

Ask about current behavior.

### Information

> Where do you record candidate information?

> What information never makes it into the ATS?

> How much candidate communication happens on WhatsApp?

> How often do you use voice notes?

### Relationship

> What happens when you haven't spoken to a candidate for six months?

> How do you prepare before reconnecting?

> Have you ever forgotten an important detail about a candidate?

### Workflow

> Walk me through your last candidate interaction.

> What did you do afterward?

> What did you have to update manually?

### Economics

> What's one candidate relationship worth to you?

> How much does your current recruiting software cost?

> What tools do you pay for?

### Product

Only after understanding the workflow:

> If software could automatically maintain a living memory of every candidate, what would you want it to know?

# 55. The Strategic Rule

For every new vertical, require:

### 1. Relationship-heavy

The professional's success depends on remembering people.

### 2. High information fragmentation

Context lives across multiple channels.

### 3. High value per relationship

One successful relationship is worth meaningful money.

### 4. Mobile-heavy

The professional interacts with people away from a desk.

### 5. Repeated interactions

The relationship evolves over time.

### 6. Existing software is insufficient

There is an ATS/CRM/system of record, but important context still escapes it.

Recruiting satisfies these exceptionally well.

Property, consulting and sales also satisfy them.

Students and generic consumers do not.
